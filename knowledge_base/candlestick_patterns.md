# Candlestick Patterns

This document outlines various candlestick patterns used in stock market analysis. Each pattern description includes:

*   **Name**: The common name of the candlestick pattern.
*   **Type**: Reversal (bullish/bearish), Continuation (bullish/bearish), or Indecision.
*   **Description**: A brief explanation of the pattern's formation and what it signifies.
*   **Interpretation**: What the pattern suggests about future price movements.
*   **Example (optional)**: A simple text-based representation or a note on how it looks.

---

## Bullish Reversal Patterns

### Hammer
*   **Type**: Bullish Reversal
*   **Description**: A small body at the upper end of the trading range with a long lower shadow (at least twice the length of the body) and little or no upper shadow. Occurs during a downtrend.
*   **Interpretation**: Suggests that sellers initially pushed prices lower, but strong buying pressure emerged to close the price near the open. Indicates potential trend reversal upwards.

### Bullish Engulfing
*   **Type**: Bullish Reversal
*   **Description**: A large bullish candle (white/green) completely engulfs the body of the previous bearish candle (black/red). Occurs during a downtrend.
*   **Interpretation**: Strong buying pressure has overcome selling pressure, indicating a potential reversal of the downtrend.

### Morning Star
*   **Type**: Bullish Reversal
*   **Description**: A three-candle pattern consisting of a long bearish candle, followed by a small-bodied candle (either bullish or bearish) that gaps down, and then a long bullish candle that closes well into the body of the first candle.
*   **Interpretation**: Indicates that the selling pressure is waning and buyers are taking control, suggesting a strong bullish reversal.

### Inverted Hammer
*   **Type**: Bullish Reversal
*   **Description**: A small body at the lower end of the trading range with a long upper shadow (at least twice the length of the body) and little or no lower shadow. Occurs during a downtrend.
*   **Interpretation**: Represents a potential bullish reversal, showing that buyers started to drive prices up even though sellers were able to push them back down before the close.

## Bearish Reversal Patterns

### Hanging Man
*   **Type**: Bearish Reversal
*   **Description**: Similar to a Hammer, but it occurs during an uptrend. A small body at the upper end of the trading range with a long lower shadow and little or no upper shadow.
*   **Interpretation**: Suggests that buyers pushed prices higher, but strong selling pressure emerged, indicating potential trend reversal downwards.

### Bearish Engulfing
*   **Type**: Bearish Reversal
*   **Description**: A large bearish candle (black/red) completely engulfs the body of the previous bullish candle (white/green). Occurs during an uptrend.
*   **Interpretation**: Strong selling pressure has overcome buying pressure, indicating a potential reversal of the uptrend.

### Evening Star
*   **Type**: Bearish Reversal
*   **Description**: A three-candle pattern consisting of a long bullish candle, followed by a small-bodied candle that gaps up, and then a long bearish candle that closes well into the body of the first candle.
*   **Interpretation**: Indicates that buying momentum is fading and sellers are seizing control, indicating a strong bearish reversal.

### Shooting Star
*   **Type**: Bearish Reversal
*   **Description**: A small body at the lower end of the trading range with a long upper shadow (at least twice the length of the body) and little or no lower shadow. Occurs during an uptrend.
*   **Interpretation**: Suggests that buyers initially pushed prices higher, but sellers took control and forced prices back down, indicating potential downward reversal.

## Continuation Patterns

### Three White Soldiers
*   **Type**: Bullish Continuation
*   **Description**: Three consecutive long-bodied bullish candles that close higher than the previous day's close. Each candle opens within the body of the previous candle. Occurs after a period of consolidation or a brief pullback in an uptrend.
*   **Interpretation**: Strong and sustained buying pressure, indicating a continuation of the bullish trend.

### Three Black Crows
*   **Type**: Bearish Continuation
*   **Description**: Three consecutive long-bodied bearish candles that close lower than the previous day's close. Each candle opens within the body of the previous candle. Occurs after a period of consolidation or a brief rally in a downtrend.
*   **Interpretation**: Strong and sustained selling pressure, indicating a continuation of the bearish trend.

## Indecision Patterns

### Doji
*   **Type**: Indecision
*   **Description**: A candlestick with virtually identical opening and closing prices, resulting in a very small or non-existent body, with upper and lower shadows of varying lengths.
*   **Interpretation**: Signifies a state of equilibrium or indecision between buyers and sellers. While not a reversal pattern on its own, a Doji occurring at the top or bottom of a trend can often precede a reversal.

### Spinning Top
*   **Type**: Indecision
*   **Description**: A candlestick with a small real body and long upper and lower shadows of roughly equal length.
*   **Interpretation**: Represents significant indecision in the market, where both buyers and sellers tried to push prices in opposite directions but neither could sustain control by the close.
